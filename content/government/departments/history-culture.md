# History & Culture — Aringay, La Union

Coastal gateway to La Union on Lingayen Gulf, **16°24' N, 120°21' E**, elevation ~20m. Land area **84.54 km²** (5.64% of La Union). **210 km north of Manila**, ~20 km south of City of San Fernando (provincial capital).

## History

Aringay is one of La Union's oldest coastal settlements, historically a fishing and trading village along the northwestern Luzon coast. Like many Ilocos towns, it grew from Spanish-era pueblos into a municipality organized under American civil government, developing around **Poblacion** and the old **Marcos Avenue** corridor that still hosts the Municipal Hall.

## Culture & Festivals

### Kilawen Festival

Annual **Kilawen Festival** celebrates Aringay's culinary and fishing heritage — _kilawen_ (Ilocano kinilaw) showcases fresh catch from Lingayen Gulf. Street dancing, food fairs, and community competitions highlight barangay participation.

### Aringay Beach

**Aringay Beach** along Lingayen Gulf is the municipality's natural draw — grey-sand coastline, fishing grounds, and sunset views. Access via Poblacion–Samara–Dulao coastal road.

### Community life

- **Barangay fiestas** per patron saint — 24 barangays each honor their own feast day
- **Ilocano hospitality** — _panagkakasala_, _bayanihan_ clean-ups, and coastal conservation
- **Markets** — Poblacion public market for fresh fish, rice, and upland produce from Basca, Gallano, Macabato
